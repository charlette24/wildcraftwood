The best slab is not always the largest or most dramatic. It is the one whose dimensions, grain, stability, and defects align with the finished table.

## Measure the real usable area

Account for checks, taper, bark loss, and the amount of material required to flatten both faces.

## Read the grain

Grain direction often predicts where a slab may move, tear out, or reveal striking figure after surfacing.

## Match the base early

A slab and base should be designed together. Leg position affects seating, visual balance, and how the top is supported over time.
