A live-edge table begins long before finish touches wood. The most important choices concern moisture, movement, structure, and proportion.

## Start with a stable slab

Document where the slab came from, its species, thickness, moisture readings, and drying history. A beautiful slab that is still moving can become an expensive problem.

## Preserve character selectively

Natural edges, knots, checks, and color variation make each piece distinct. Not every defect should remain. Loose bark, unstable voids, and structural cracks need deliberate treatment.

## Flatten without erasing the tree

Flattening establishes a reliable reference surface. The goal is not to make the slab visually sterile; it is to create a stable plane while retaining the form that made the slab worth choosing.

## Build for seasonal movement

Bases and fasteners must allow the top to expand and contract across the grain. Good furniture respects that movement instead of trying to overpower it.
